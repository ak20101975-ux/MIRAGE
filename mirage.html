02 · AI brain

How the AI decides

Five steps run every 1.5 seconds. The highlighted card is what the AI is doing right now, with its live numbers underneath.

STEP 01

Sense

Reads algae level, temperature, pH, turbidity, light and (simulated) CO₂ for each city. Stuck or dead CO₂ sensors are detected and replaced by a model estimate.

STEP 02

Learn

Estimates each city's CO₂ production rate and keeps a 24-hour rush-hour profile (the bars on each city card).

STEP 03

Predict

Runs the city model forward (dashed amber line on the charts) to find when CO₂ will cross the trigger level and how much algae will be needed.

STEP 04

Decide

Receivers are ranked by urgency × population. Donors may never drop below the reserve level or below what they need themselves.

STEP 05

Act

Pump power scales with urgency; pump time = litres × s/L ÷ power. Pre-pumping starts early enough for the algae to take effect. Pumping stops when the deficit closes.

05 · Science & model

The model behind every number

What comes from the project guide, what is an assumption, and checks that prove the simulation behaves correctly.

∑Equations

dC/dt = E(t) − kv·(C − Cbg) − a·m·S·(C − Cbg) // city CO₂, ppm per sim-minute
m = health × light factor // health = f(temp)·f(pH)·f(turbidity)
dS/dt = r·S·(1 − S/Smax) ± pump flow // algae stock, litres per sim-minute
Sneed = (E ÷ (target − Cbg) − kv) ÷ (a·m) // algae that hold the target
pump time = litres × s/L ÷ power, power = 55% + 45% × urgency, PWM = power × max PWM

C = CO₂ (ppm), E = the city's CO₂ production (ppm/min), S = algae stock (L), m = how effectively the algae work right now (0–1). Transferred algae change S only. CO₂ then changes step by step, so it never drops instantly. 1 simulated day = 4 real minutes at 1× (guide §5).

⚠Parameters the guide does not give ASSUMPTIONS

Illustrative calibration choices, not measurements. Edit them to test sensitivity. Starred items apply on Reset.

✓Model checks

Runs the model on hidden copies of the cities and tests the equations, safety rules, both transfer directions, sensor-fault handling, the energy balance, and that CO₂ changes gradually after a transfer.

ⓘHonest limits

  • CO₂, algae, sensor readings, energy and all impacts are simulated model-scale values, not experimental results.
  • Only the AI settings come from the guide. Absorption, growth, air exchange, tank size, emission patterns, algae-health ranges, light response, population, solar and battery sizes are assumptions.
  • Not modelled: O₂ production (the guide gives no yield), nutrient limits, and pH or turbidity changes caused by CO₂ uptake.
  • Uptake is assumed proportional to algae stock × excess CO₂ × health × light factor; growth is a simple logistic curve.
  • While a CO₂ sensor is faulty, the AI estimates CO₂ from its learned pattern, so it cannot see a new pollution spike in that city.
  • The AI is rule-based with statistical pattern learning (moving averages). It does not use machine learning.
  • Pump seconds use the guide's calibration (0.35 s per simulated litre): demo-scale times, not real pipe flow.

Wiring notes
  1. Upload algaegrid.ino to the Arduino. Close the Serial Monitor afterwards (it blocks the port).
  2. Open this page in Chrome or Edge. Untick Demo mode or press Connect Arduino, then choose the Arduino's COM port. With Auto-connect on, you only choose it once.
  3. Pump 1 moves algae B → A. Pump 2 moves A → B. Use Test pump first, then tune Pump time per litre so the demo looks right.
  4. Both L298N grounds and the Arduino GND must be joined (with the supply minus). Remove the ENA jumper on each L298N so PWM works.
  5. The L298N drops about 2 V: use a supply about 2 V above the pumps' rating, or lower Max PWM.

Without the Arduino, Demo mode drives the same virtual pins shown on the left. At speeds other than 1×, the real pump time is scaled to match the on-screen pump.
